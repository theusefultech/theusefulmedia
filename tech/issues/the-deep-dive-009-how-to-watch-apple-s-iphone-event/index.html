How to watch Apple's iPhone event

Five things that will actually matter, and three that will sound bigger than they are

👋 HEY, IT'S RAJA

Apple's iPhone event, "Surprise and Shine," takes place on Wednesday, September 9 at 10:00am Pacific. That is 1:00pm US eastern, 6:00pm in the UK, and 10:30pm in India. It streams on Apple's website, the Apple TV app, and YouTube.

This issue goes out before it starts, which is deliberate. Every outlet will tell you what was announced by Thursday morning. Far fewer will tell you beforehand which parts to pay attention to, and that is a more useful thing to have in your head while you watch.

So: five things that will genuinely matter, three that will sound enormous on stage and will not change your life, and what to do afterwards.

⚠️ Everything below is based on reporting and leaks, not confirmation. Some of it will be wrong by Thursday. I will send the verdict on Friday once we have facts instead of rumours.

📘 Watching Apple's iPhone event properly

What to listen for, what to discount, and the sentence Apple will not say clearly.

Five things that actually matter

1. There is probably no standard iPhone for you this year

This is the single most important thing and Apple will not put it on a slide.

Reporting consistently says Apple is splitting its release schedule for the first time. September gets the iPhone 18 Pro, the 18 Pro Max, and the first foldable. The regular iPhone 18, the cheaper 18e, and the second-generation iPhone Air are all held back to around spring 2027.

If you buy the standard model, which is what most sensible people do, there may be nothing for you at this event. Your options become a Pro, a discounted iPhone 17, or waiting about six months. Watch for whether Apple addresses this directly or simply moves past it.

2. The prices are going up

Price rises are widely expected, driven by an industry-wide memory shortage rather than anything Apple decided on a whim.

The Mac line is the evidence you can already check. The Mac mini was $599 in May, went to $799 in June when Apple raised prices across the range over component costs, and reached $899 with last month's M6 model. Same pressure, same direction.

Listen for whether Apple states prices plainly or leans on monthly instalment figures and carrier trade-in numbers. The framing tells you how big the increase is.

3. Siri AI is not arriving with iOS 27

Apple has been demonstrating a substantially better Siri for over a year, and it will be prominent on stage. It is not shipping with the software next week.

Reporting indicates a Siri AI beta in English later in 2026, not at the iOS 27 release, and it will not be available in the European Union or China at launch. If you are in the EU, that is not a delay, it is an absence.

When you hear Siri features described, listen for the tense. "Coming later this year" is doing a great deal of work in that sentence.

4. The foldable will be genuinely hard to buy

The folding iPhone, reportedly called iPhone Ultra internally, is the reason this event exists. It is also reportedly supply-constrained, with estimates of only 500,000 to a million units assembled in the quarter.

Reported specifications are striking: around 4.5mm thick unfolded, which would make it the thinnest product Apple has ever shipped, a liquid-metal hinge, a titanium and aluminium frame, a crease under 0.15mm deep, and a roughly 7.6 to 7.8-inch inner display. Leaked prices land somewhere between $1,949 and $2,320 for the entry configuration.

If you want one, the decision is made on pre-order morning, not after a fortnight of reviews.

5. This is the new CEO's first keynote

The genuinely historic part, and the part that will get the least coverage.

John Ternus became Apple's CEO on September 1, with Tim Cook moving to Executive Chairman. Ternus joined Apple in 2001, ran hardware engineering, and at 51 could plausibly lead the company for a decade or more. Johny Srouji, the architect of Apple's silicon, moved up to chief hardware officer alongside him.

Only four people have ever run Apple. Whatever gets announced, watch how he does it. That is the thing you will still be thinking about in five years.

Three things that will sound bigger than they are

The chip percentages. The A20 Pro is reported to be Apple's first 2nm phone chip, roughly 18% faster and 30% more power efficient than the A19 Pro. Both numbers are real and neither is perceptible in ordinary use. The efficiency figure matters more than the speed one, because it shows up as battery life. The speed figure will not change your day.

The camera demonstration. Expect a variable aperture rear camera on the Pro models and possibly 6x optical zoom on the Pro Max, shown on a cinema screen in perfect light with a professional operating it. Ask yourself what you actually photograph. If the answer is family, food, and documents, a new sensor changes very little.

The word "available." Apple uses "available today," "available this fall," and "coming later this year" to mean three quite different things, delivered in the same tone. When something excites you, note which phrase was attached to it.

What to do afterwards

iOS 27 is expected to reach everyone on Monday, September 14, with the release candidate likely arriving right after the event. It runs on the iPhone 11 and later, though Apple Intelligence needs an iPhone 15 Pro or newer.

Three things worth doing between now and then: back up properly, check you have the storage headroom for a major update, and resist installing it on day one if your phone is the one you work from. Nothing in iOS 27 is worth a broken Tuesday.
