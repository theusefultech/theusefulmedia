I overthink everything

Overthinking feels like solving a problem, but it rarely moves anything. These 3 books cut it differently: question the story you are telling yourself, turn the worry into 1 next action, or accept that you can't be sure about the future.

What does your overthinking look like?

Fix 1 · Meditations · Marcus Aurelius

It's your judgment that hurts, not the event

Marcus keeps reminding himself that outside events don't upset us. Our opinion about them does, and that opinion is something we can drop. Separate what happened from the story you added to it.

A theme across the book

Try it today

Write the thing on your mind in 2 lines: what actually happened, and what you are telling yourself it means.

Best if: You replay a moment and keep adding meaning to it.

Fix 2 · The Obstacle Is the Way · Ryan Holiday

Turn the worry into the next step

Holiday borrows from the Stoics: see the problem clearly, then act on the part you can move. Thinking in circles feels like work, but only the next concrete step changes anything.

The discipline of action

Try it today

Write the 1 next action for the thing you keep thinking about. Do it in the next hour, even a small version.

Best if: You think about a problem for days but don't start on it.

Which chapters to readGet the book

Fix 3 · Four Thousand Weeks · Oliver Burkeman

Stop asking the future for a guarantee

Burkeman argues that much worry is an attempt to feel in control of a future that no one controls. You can plan, but you can't get certainty in advance. Making peace with that leaves more attention for today.

On the need for certainty

Try it today

Next time you worry about a what if, say: I can't know this yet. Then plan only the part that is due today.

Best if: You worry about what might go wrong next week or next year.

Quick answers

How do I stop overthinking?

Separate what happened from the story you added (Meditations), write 1 next action and do it (The Obstacle Is the Way), and stop asking the future for certainty you can't get (Four Thousand Weeks).

Which book is best for overthinking?

Start with Meditations if you replay the past, The Obstacle Is the Way if you never act, and Four Thousand Weeks if you worry about the future.
