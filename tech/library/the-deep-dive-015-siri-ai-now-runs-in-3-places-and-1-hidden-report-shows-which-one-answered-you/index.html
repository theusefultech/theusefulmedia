Siri AI now runs in 3 places, and 1 hidden report shows which one answered you

On your device, on Apple's servers, and now on Google Cloud

THE USEFUL TECH

📘 THE DEEP DIVE · WEDNESDAY TUTORIAL · ISSUE #015

👋 HEY, IT'S RAJA

Ask Siri AI something and the answer can come from 3 different places: your own device, Apple's servers, or, since June, servers Apple uses inside Google Cloud.

Apple keeps a log of which one handled each request, 3 taps deep in Settings. Today, what each place means for your data, how to read that log, and the apps that never send anything anywhere.

📘 Where Siri AI actually answers you

On-device, Private Cloud Compute, and now Google Cloud.

#1 On your device

Simple requests run on the Apple Intelligence model built into your iPhone or Mac. Nothing leaves the device, and it works without a connection.

How much stays local depends on your hardware. On the Mac, the newer, more capable on-device model needs an M3 chip or later with 12GB of memory. Older Macs hand more of the work to the cloud.

#2 Private Cloud Compute

Bigger requests go to Apple's own servers, which run on Apple silicon. Apple's rules for them are strict: your data is used only for that one request, it isn't stored, and independent researchers can inspect the software the servers run.

#3 Google Cloud

On 8 June, Apple said some Private Cloud Compute work now runs on Google Cloud servers with NVIDIA GPUs, using NVIDIA's Confidential Computing. Apple also said the full set of protections would be added gradually during the beta.

It still runs under the Private Cloud Compute name and rules. The difference is that the hardware sits in Google's data centres instead of Apple's.

How to see where your requests went

On a Mac:

  1. Open System Settings and click Privacy & Security.
  2. Scroll down and click Apple Intelligence Report.
  3. Choose Last 15 minutes or Last 7 days.
  4. Click Export Activity. It saves a file called Apple_Intelligence_Report.json.

On an iPhone: Settings > Privacy & Security > Apple Intelligence Report, with the same 2 time ranges.

What's inside the file

Each request is listed with whether it ran on your device or on Private Cloud Compute. TidBITS found the file also holds the hidden instructions Apple sends along with your request, and the longer answers Siri drafted but didn't read out to you.

It also contains your own requests and personal details, so remove those before you share it with anyone.

💡 Tip: Export the 7-day report on an ordinary weekday. You'll see how much of what you ask actually leaves your device.
