The Mac app that remembers everything you copy

A clipboard manager that turns copy and paste into an archive you can search

👋 HEY, IT'S RAJA

Copy a link. Copy something else before you use it. The first thing is gone.

Every Mac has worked this way since 1984, and most of us have quietly built habits around it. Pasting things into a scratch note so they survive. Opening a tab we do not need yet. Retyping an address we already had five minutes ago.

Paste fixes it, and it is the app I want to put in front of you this week.

🔍 The find: Paste

A clipboard manager for Mac, iPhone and iPad that keeps everything you copy instead of one thing at a time.

What it is

Paste sits behind your existing copy and paste. You use the same shortcuts you already use, and Paste quietly keeps a record of everything that passes through. Text, links, images, files, all of it lands in a timeline you can scroll back through.

It has been around for over ten years, Apple has featured it in multiple categories, and it runs across Mac, iPhone and iPad.

What it actually does

Infinite history. Everything you copy goes into a visual timeline you can scroll and filter by the app it came from or the device it came from. Nothing falls off the end.

Power Search. Search across everything you have ever copied, by content, by app, or by device. The thing you copied out of an email three weeks ago is still findable in seconds, which is the feature that turns the app from a convenience into a genuine archive.

Pinboards. Folders for the things you paste constantly. Email templates, code snippets, your own bio, project links, the reply you send forty times a month. Pin them once and they stay one keystroke away.

Sync. Copy on the iPhone, paste on the Mac. Everything moves through your own iCloud, so the clipboard follows you across devices.

Privacy. Your clipboard stays on your device and in your private iCloud. Paste does not send it anywhere and does not see what you copy, and you control what gets saved. For an app that by definition touches passwords, card numbers and half-finished messages, that architecture is the part worth knowing about.

Why you would install it

Clipboard managers sound like a solution to a problem nobody has, right up to the first time you scroll back through a day of your own copying and see how much of it you had already lost.

Developers keep commands and snippets available across a whole session. Designers keep colour codes in Pinboards by project. Anyone who writes keeps research, drafts and links from disappearing the moment something else gets copied. And everybody gets the ordinary version: the address, the phone number, the tracking link, still there when you come back for it.

💡 Pro tip: set up two or three Pinboards on day one rather than waiting until you need them. Your email signature, your most-sent reply, and whatever link you paste every week. That is the point where a clipboard manager stops being a safety net and starts saving you real time.

💻 Platform: Mac, iPhone, iPad

☁️ Sync: your own iCloud, nothing sent to external servers

🔎 Best feature: Power Search across everything you have ever copied

📌 Second best: Pinboards for the things you paste constantly
