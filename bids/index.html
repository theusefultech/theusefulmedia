        Technical proposal

        Written in the order FAR 52.212-2 lists the evaluation factors, so each numbered heading matches a factor the evaluators score. Shortened to fit this page.

        1. Technical approach

        1.1 Schedule. Sample Cleaning Co. cleans the Manchester Ranger District Office on Tuesdays and Fridays between 4:00 and 10:00 pm, and the Mt. Tabor Administrative Building on Wednesdays between 10:00 am and 4:00 pm SOW 200.10. From May 1 through October 31 the Mt. Tabor Quarters gets 3 visits a week: standard cleans on Mondays and Thursdays and the intensive kitchen and restroom clean on Wednesdays SOW 222.0. Monthly tasks ride with the first visit of each month 220.2. Semi-annual work is booked for the last 2 weeks of April and October, with carpet dates agreed with the COR 2 weeks ahead 220.3, 220.32.

        1.2 Requirements we cannot meet. None. 1 item waits on the COR: the Visitor Center floor is slate, and any stone-safe product needs COR approval before use 220.16. We send the safety data sheet for our pH-neutral stone cleaner in week 1.

        1.3 Quality Control Plan. The site supervisor checks every visit against the Attachment 8 checklist and signs it before leaving SOW 262. Restrooms carry a 100% quality level in Attachment 7, so they get 2 checks: the cleaner, then the supervisor. A deficiency the COR reports is answered the same business day and corrected on the next visit, or within 10 business days for semi-annual work SOW 263. Each Friday the supervisor reads the week's checklists for repeat misses and retrains on site.

        2. Project methodology

        2.1 Personnel. Site Manager / Supervisor: 9 years running janitorial crews, 4 of them at a county courthouse. Assigned personnel: 2 cleaners, each with the firm more than 3 years. All 3 complete the background check forms at award and work business hours until cleared SOW 140.

        2.2 Equipment and 2.3 Materials. Listed on the Attachment 10 forms, with each biobased product marked against the Attachment 6 categories.

        3. Subcontractor retention and workforce stability plan

        a. Pay. Wage determination 2015-4139, revision 35, sets janitors at $19.35 an hour plus $5.92 in health and welfare. We pay $21.50. The SOW itself reports 2.1 to 2.7% unemployment around Manchester and rents of $1,700 to $3,200 a month SOW 110.

        b. Retention. A $250 bonus at 6 and 12 months for cleaners with no unplanned absences.

        c. Supervision. 1 supervisor to 2 cleaners, on site every week.

        d. Backup labor. 2 trained on-call cleaners who live within 20 miles cover a call-out the same day. No work is subcontracted, so no partner reaches the 20% line that triggers a consent letter.

        Questions contractors ask

        Where does the radar come from?

        2 public federal sources. Live bids come from SAM.gov's file of active contract opportunities. Contract holders, values and end dates come from USAspending.gov award records. The radar was last refreshed on November 27, 2026.

        Does an ending term mean a new bid is coming?

        Sometimes. Agencies extend many contracts through option years, and some work stops. The radar shows the date the current term ends and nothing more, so you know which offices to watch and who holds the work today.

        What if I read the package and decide not to bid?

        Then there is no invoice. Tell me what made it a no, and that is the end of it.

        Will you tell me what to charge?

        No. The pricing worksheet lists the solicitation's line items and shows what the current contract paid when public records have it. The number is yours.
