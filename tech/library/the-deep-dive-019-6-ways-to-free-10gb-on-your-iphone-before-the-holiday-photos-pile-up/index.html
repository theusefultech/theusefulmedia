6 ways to free 10GB on your iPhone before the holiday photos pile up

3 settings already on your phone and 3 apps that do what the settings can't.

THE USEFUL TECH

📘 THE DEEP DIVE · WEDNESDAY GUIDE · ISSUE #019

👋 HEY, IT'S RAJA

December is the month your camera roll grows fastest. Family dinners, presents being opened, 40 shots of the same tree because 1 of them might be the one. If your iPhone is already close to full, that's when the Storage Almost Full warning shows up, usually halfway through a video.

Here are 6 ways to win back space before that happens. The first 3 are settings already on your phone. The last 3 are apps that do what the settings can't.

Start by checking where you stand: Settings, General, iPhone Storage. The bar at the top shows what's using the space, and every app below is sorted by size.

1️⃣ Empty Recently Deleted

Photos you delete aren't gone yet. They sit in the Recently Deleted album for 30 days and still take up space. Open Photos, scroll down to Utilities, open Recently Deleted, then tap Select and Delete All. If you've been cleaning up for a while, this alone can give back a few gigabytes.

2️⃣ Let Messages forget old attachments

Every photo, video and voice note anyone has ever sent you is still in Messages. In iPhone Storage, tap Messages and use Review Large Attachments to delete the biggest ones.

Then go to Settings, Apps, Messages and set Keep Messages to 1 Year so it never piles up again. That deletes conversations older than a year, so screenshot anything you want to keep first.

3️⃣ Offload the apps you haven't opened since summer

Offloading removes an app but keeps its data, so if you reinstall it, everything's where you left it. In iPhone Storage, tap any app you never use and choose Offload App. Or go to Settings, Apps, App Store and turn on Offload Unused Apps, and your iPhone does it for you when space runs low.

4️⃣ Swipe through your camera roll with SwipeClean

Photos finds exact duplicates, but it won't tell you that 2,000 of your pictures are screenshots, blurry shots and near copies of the same moment. SwipeClean, an indie app from Anthony Cooke, finds those for you and turns the cleanup into a game: swipe right to keep, left to delete.

It groups duplicates, blurry photos, forgotten screenshots and similar shots, and it flags Live Photos that quietly use extra space. You can go through a full month of your photos free before you decide, and unlocking the rest is a one-time purchase, not the weekly subscription most swipe cleaners charge.

Get SwipeClean

5️⃣ Shrink videos without deleting them with HEVCut

Videos are usually the biggest thing on your iPhone, and deleting them is the hard part. HEVCut re-saves your photos and videos in Apple's smaller HEVC and HEIC formats. When 9to5Mac featured it in October, it saved 50% to 80% on each file without much loss in quality.

Everything runs on your iPhone, so nothing is uploaded. It also finds duplicates, cleans up burst shots and Live Photos, and can export compressed videos straight to an SD card or external drive.

Get HEVCut

6️⃣ Move the originals off your phone with PhotoSync

The safest space to free is the space taken by photos already backed up somewhere else. PhotoSync has been doing this since 2011 and has about 38,000 ratings averaging 4.7. It sends photos and videos from your iPhone over Wi-Fi to your Mac or PC, a NAS, a USB drive, or services like Dropbox, OneDrive and Google Photos, with no iCloud in the middle.

The free version lets you test everything but sends lower-quality copies. The Pro upgrade is a one-time purchase that keeps full quality, RAW files included. Premium adds Autotransfer, which backs up new photos in the background when you get home or plug in to charge.

Get PhotoSync

Tip: do #6 before #4 and #5. Back up the originals first, then clean and compress, so nothing you delete is the only copy.
