5 new iPhone and Mac apps, from a Knives Out camera to a page turner that listens

A director's camera app, a Mac data monitor, a Finder replacement and 2 more.

👋 HEY, IT'S RAJA

Black Friday is 27 November, and every app deal I've confirmed is now in 1 list (more on that below). But first, 5 new apps that showed up in the last few weeks, before they get buried under sale announcements.

3 are for iPhone, 2 are for Mac, and 1 was built by a 15-year-old classical guitarist.

1of 5

Chromagraph Camera, a film director's iPhone camera (iPhone)

Rian Johnson, who directed Knives Out, asked his longtime cinematographer Steve Yedlin to build him the iPhone camera app he wanted.

Chromagraph CameraA RAW iPhone camera with a cinematographer's lookPrice and alternatives

Chromagraph Camera, made by Yedlin and color science programmer Eric Cameron, shoots RAW, skips the iPhone's own look processing and uses the same image processing they use on their films instead.

It isn't a filter on top of an already processed photo.

It builds the picture from the raw sensor data, keeps the full RAW so you can rework it later, and can save a JPEG or HEIC straight to your library.

It shoots photos only, no video, and it jumped to the top of the paid Photo and Video chart after launch. You pay once.

Get Chromagraph Camera

2of 5

Kilobat, see which apps are eating your data (Mac)

Kilobat is a menu bar app that shows live traffic for every app on your Mac. On a phone hotspot, it can stop background apps from burning through your data, and it tells you what each network has cost you.

KilobatSee which Mac apps use your network and block themPrice and alternatives

It also answers "why is the internet slow?" with speed and stutter tests and a Why can't it connect? check that finds the layer that's broken.

If you run long downloads or AI tools like Claude Code, it keeps your Mac awake until the work is done, then lets it sleep. Live monitoring is free, and Pro is a one-time purchase.

Get Kilobat

3of 5

CueFlip, a page turner that listens while you play (iPhone and iPad)

Yuze Pan, a 15-year-old classical guitarist, built CueFlip because turning the page meant taking a hand off the guitar.

You teach it how you play a piece, from a live rehearsal or a recording, mark where the turns go, and it turns the page for you as it listens.

Wink your right eye to go forward and your left to go back, or pair a Bluetooth page turner.

Pro adds a dictionary of more than 20,000 musical terms with translation in 13 languages, pulled straight from your scanned scores. The free version turns pages automatically for 1 score.

Find CueFlip on the App Store

4of 5

Iruka, a Finder replacement with a terminal that follows you (Mac)

Iruka is a native Mac file manager, written in Swift and inspired by Dolphin on Linux. Its headline feature is a terminal built into the window that follows you around: click into a folder and the command line is already there, no typing cd.

It previews code with syntax highlighting and shows CSV files as a grid, which Quick Look can't, and it adds git, SSH and search with grep and regex.

It's a one-time purchase with a 30-day free trial. If you live in Terminal, this is the one to try.

Try Iruka free

5of 5

Sensonym, learn a language by doing it (iPhone)

Sensonym teaches vocabulary through short mystery stories where you discover each word with your hands. In a bakery story, you blow at your phone to cool a loaf and uncover heiß, German for hot, then wipe the counter on screen to reveal putzen, to clean.

SensonymLearn words in 10 languages by moving your phonePrice and alternatives

Every word is tied to 1 of more than 40 physical actions, like tilting, whispering and tapping, so it sticks to something you did, not just something you read. It covers German, Spanish, Korean and more, and it's free to start.

Get Sensonym

Nitro Photo goes 50% off lifetime from 22 November.

Nitro Photo is a Mac photo editor that works inside Apple Photos and Finder, so you edit where your pictures already live instead of importing them somewhere new. It handles RAW files and LUTs, and 1 purchase covers every Apple device it runs on.

From 22 November to 4 December, the lifetime license is 50% off. There's a 7-day free trial, so you can test it on your own photos before the sale starts.
