Set up a parent's iPhone in 20 minutes, with the exact taps for 7 changes

Bigger text, scam call screening, Medical ID and 4 more changes most parents' iPhones are missing.

👋 HEY, IT'S RAJA

If you're seeing family this week, someone will hand you their iPhone and ask why it keeps doing that. For a lot of us, that someone is a parent.

This is the 20 minutes I'd spend on a parent's iPhone. 7 changes, each with the exact taps, in the order that matters most. Most of them are settings already on the phone, so there's nothing to buy.

A quick word from today's ad partner, then the 7 changes.

FOR YOUR SETUP ·

1of 7

Make everything bigger (2 minutes)

Go to Settings, Display & Brightness, Text Size, and drag the slider 1 or 2 steps right. Turn on Bold Text on the same screen.

If icons and buttons still feel small, open Display Zoom on that screen and pick Larger Text. That makes the whole interface bigger, not just the words.

2of 7

Stop scam calls before the phone rings (2 minutes)

Go to Settings, Apps, Phone, and under Screen Unknown Callers, tap Ask Reason for Calling.

Now anyone who isn't in their contacts has to say who they are and why they're calling before the phone rings, and your parent reads the answer on screen. The doctor's office gets through.

Most robocallers hang up. People in their contacts ring as normal, so add the numbers that matter first. It needs iOS 26 or later.

3of 7

Fill in Medical ID and Emergency SOS (4 minutes)

Open the Health app, tap their profile picture, then Medical ID, then Edit. Add medications, allergies and conditions, add you as an emergency contact, and turn on Show When Locked so a paramedic can read it without the passcode.

Then go to Settings, Emergency SOS and check how it's set to call. Show them once: hold the side button and a volume button, and the iPhone calls emergency services and texts their emergency contacts their location.

4of 7

Make yourself their recovery contact (3 minutes)

Go to Settings, tap their name at the top, then Sign-In & Security, then Account Recovery, then Add Recovery Contact, and pick yourself.

If they ever forget their Apple Account password, your iPhone shows a code that gets them back in, instead of a long lockout and a call to Apple.

5of 7

Turn on Assistive Access if the iPhone overwhelms them (5 minutes)

This is the one most people have never heard of. Assistive Access turns the iPhone into a few big buttons: Calls, Messages, Camera and Photos, with only the contacts you choose.

Go to Settings, Accessibility, Assistive Access, tap Set Up Assistive Access, pick the apps, then set a passcode. To leave it, triple-click the side button and enter that passcode.

Apple built it for people with cognitive disabilities, and it works just as well for anyone who gets lost 2 taps into a normal iPhone.

6of 7

Put the grandkids on their screen without teaching them anything (2 minutes)

Make a Shared Album in Photos and invite them, and every photo you add shows up on their iPhone.

If they have an old iPad sitting in a drawer, Digital Photo Frame App turns it into a frame that plays that shared album and adds new photos the moment you post them.

The download is free.

7of 7

Captions for the conversations they miss (2 minutes)

If hearing is getting harder, Hearing Buddy puts live captions of what people around them say on their iPhone or iPad, free and without limits, and it works offline.

Prop it on the dinner table and they can follow the conversation as text.

For small print on menus and medicine boxes, add the built-in Magnifier to Control Center, or install Be My Eyes, a free app that describes what the camera sees.

Tip: do every step on their phone while they hold it, and say where each setting lives out loud. They'll remember the place, even if they forget the name.
