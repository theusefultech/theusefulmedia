Who Apple's new Siri AI is actually for

The early reviews split along one clean line, and it tells you whether this is worth your attention

👋 HEY, IT'S RAJA

iOS 27 landed on Monday, and the question everybody is asking about Siri AI is whether it is any good.

That is the wrong question, and the reviews prove it. Read enough of them and you find something odd: they do not disagree about what Siri AI does. They agree almost perfectly on the facts and then reach opposite conclusions. Two writers at the same publication, on the same day, published takes that barely sound like the same product.

Once you see what actually divides them, the whole thing becomes simple to reason about.

📘 Who Siri AI is actually for

The line the reviews split along, and which side of it you are on.

The split

It is not iPhone versus Android, or optimist versus cynic. It is this: had you already been using an AI assistant before Monday?

Reviewers who had not are close to astonished. One hands-on described Siri getting a brain transplant, and for someone whose last Siri interaction was a mangled timer request, that is exactly what it feels like. It understands follow-up questions. It searches your own photos and messages. It stops pretending it did not hear you.

Reviewers who already live in ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude came away shrugging. The reasoning is shallower than what they use daily, long or messy requests lose the thread, and nothing here is new to them. One reviewer put it as: great for longtime iPhone users, a little short for everyone else.

My favourite summary came from a YouTuber, and it is the most useful sentence written about this launch. To win people over, Siri does not need to be powerful. It needs to be available and easy. That is the entire product strategy, and judged against it Apple has done fine.

What it is genuinely good at

Three things stand out, and they share a quality worth naming.

Finding your own things. Digging out a specific photo, a detail buried in an old thread, a delivery confirmation you half remember. This is the thing no outside chatbot can do, because they cannot see your phone.

Acting inside apps. Pick an image, tell it what to do with the image, and have it done. Onscreen awareness plus app actions means the assistant can finish the job rather than describe how.

Being there. "Hey Siri", the side button, or a swipe down from the Dynamic Island. No app to open, no tab to find. There is also a standalone Siri app now, with conversation history that syncs across your devices.

Notice the pattern. Everything Siri AI is good at depends on being inside your phone. None of it depends on being clever.

Where it falls short

Reasoning depth is the honest one. If your work involves thinking through something complicated, drafting at length, or holding a long messy problem together, this is not that tool and does not claim to be.

Third-party apps are the practical one. One reviewer found Siri could only surface delivery messages when using Apple's own Mail app rather than Gmail. If your life runs through non-Apple apps, a good deal of the personal context magic simply does not reach you.

And the Dynamic Island swipe is reportedly cramped, since swiping down already gives you Control Center on one side and notifications on the other. A third gesture up there is a lot.

The constraints nobody puts in the headline

  • It ships as a beta, and Apple has not said when it leaves beta.
  • English only at launch. French, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese and Spanish arrive in October.
  • Requires an iPhone 15 Pro, 15 Pro Max, or any iPhone 16 and later.
  • Not available in the European Union or China.
  • It runs on Apple's own models combined with Google's Gemini, through Private Cloud Compute.

And the one that should be the headline

Apple has confirmed daily usage limits on the Siri AI features that rely on server-side models, with expanded paid access reported for 2027.

Sit with that. The flagship feature of the operating system, on hardware you have already paid for, is metered. And there is a subscription coming.

This is not outrageous, because running these models genuinely costs money per query and every other assistant works the same way. But it is a real change in what owning an iPhone includes, and it deserved more than the paragraph it received.

So which are you?

If you have never used an AI assistant: this is a significant upgrade and you should use it. It is free, it is already on your phone, and it is genuinely better at the things you were already trying to do. Start with finding your own stuff, because that is where it shines.

If you already use Claude or ChatGPT daily: do not expect to be impressed, and do not write it off either. Siri AI is not competing with your assistant. It is doing a different job.

The division of labour I have settled on

Siri gets anything that needs to be inside the phone. Find that email. What does this screen say. Add that to Thursday. Where is the photo of the receipt. These are retrieval and action tasks, and Siri wins them by default because nothing else has the access.

Claude gets anything that needs thinking. Drafting, deciding, working through a problem with more than one step, anything where the answer is not sitting somewhere on my device waiting to be found.

The mistake is treating them as rivals and then being disappointed in whichever one you picked. They are not rivals. One knows where everything is and the other knows what to do about it.
